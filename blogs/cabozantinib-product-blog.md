## Description

Cabozantinib is the generic name of the medicine. **Cabometyx** and **Cometriq** are its brand names, made by Exelixis (and marketed by Ipsen outside the US and Japan).

Cabozantinib is a **tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI)**. Chemotherapy attacks healthy and cancer cells alike. Cabozantinib instead blocks several specific proteins that tumours rely on, mainly **MET, VEGFR-2, AXL and RET**. These proteins help cancer cells grow and build new blood vessels to feed themselves. When they are blocked, the tumour's growth can slow down or stop.

The FDA has approved **Cabometyx (tablets)** for:

- **Advanced renal cell carcinoma (kidney cancer)**, on its own or as a first treatment together with nivolumab
- **Hepatocellular carcinoma (liver cancer)** in patients who have already been treated with sorafenib
- **Differentiated thyroid cancer** that has worsened after other targeted therapy and no longer responds to radioactive iodine (ages 12 and up)
- **Pancreatic and extra-pancreatic neuroendocrine tumours** that were treated before (ages 12 and up; approved in March 2025)

**Cometriq (capsules)** is approved for **metastatic medullary thyroid cancer**.

Cabometyx and Cometriq contain the same medicine but are **not interchangeable**, so never switch between them on your own.

## Usage and Dosage

Cabozantinib is taken by mouth once a day. Your oncologist chooses the dose based on your cancer type, liver function and other medicines.

- **Cabometyx alone (adults):** 60 mg once daily
- **Cabometyx with nivolumab (kidney cancer):** 40 mg once daily
- **Children 12 and older (under 40 kg):** 40 mg once daily
- **Cometriq (medullary thyroid cancer):** 140 mg once daily
- **Moderate liver impairment:** 40 mg once daily (Cabometyx)

**How to take it:**

- Take Cabometyx on an empty stomach, at least **1 hour before or 2 hours after** eating.
- For Cometriq, do not eat for **2 hours before and 1 hour after** each dose.
- Swallow the tablet whole with water. Do not crush or chew it.
- **Missed a dose?** If your next dose is less than 12 hours away, skip the missed one. Never take two doses together.
- **Avoid grapefruit and grapefruit juice.** They can raise cabozantinib levels in your blood.

**Caution:** strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (such as ketoconazole) raise cabozantinib levels, and strong inducers (such as rifampin) lower them. Your oncologist may change your dose if you need these medicines. Only your oncologist should change your dose.

## Side Effects

Most patients have some side effects, but many can be managed by pausing treatment or lowering the dose.

**Common side effects include:**

- Diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting and constipation
- Tiredness and weakness
- Loss of appetite and weight loss
- Hand-foot syndrome (red, sore or blistered palms and soles)
- High blood pressure
- Mouth sores and changes in taste
- Hoarse voice and changes in hair colour

**Serious side effects that need immediate medical attention:**

- Severe bleeding, such as vomiting blood, coughing up blood or black stools
- A tear in the stomach or bowel wall, or an abnormal connection between organs (fistula)
- Blood clots, stroke or heart attack
- Very high blood pressure
- Jaw bone damage (osteonecrosis of the jaw)
- Protein in the urine and liver damage
- RPLS, a rare brain condition that causes headache, confusion, seizures or vision changes

That is why your doctor will check your blood pressure, liver, kidneys and thyroid regularly.

## Warnings and Precautions

Tell your oncologist about all your health conditions and medicines, including supplements, before you start.

- **Surgery:** stop at least **3 weeks before planned surgery**. Restart no sooner than **2 weeks after major surgery**, and only once the wound has healed.
- **Dental procedures:** stop at least **3 weeks before invasive dental work** to lower the risk of jaw damage.
- **Recent bleeding:** do not start cabozantinib after a recent serious bleed.
- **Blood pressure:** it must be under control before treatment begins.
- **Adrenal problems:** these have been reported when cabozantinib is combined with nivolumab.
- **Pregnancy:** cabozantinib can harm an unborn baby. Use effective birth control during treatment and for **4 months after the last dose**.
- **Supplements:** avoid St John's wort, which can make cabozantinib less effective.

## Generic vs Brand

The brands Cabometyx and Cometriq are made by Exelixis. At present, the **US FDA has not approved a generic version of Cabometyx**, so patients in the United States can only buy the brand.

In India, several companies make generic cabozantinib under Indian regulatory approval. They include Natco (Cazanat), Dr. Reddy's and Cipla. They contain the same active ingredient, usually as 20 mg, 40 mg and 60 mg tablets, at a small fraction of the brand's price.

In other words, a generic can make long-term treatment affordable. Check with your oncologist before switching.

## Safety Advice

- **Alcohol:** No direct interaction is known, but alcohol can strain the liver and worsen stomach side effects. Limit or avoid it.
- **Pregnancy:** Can harm an unborn baby. Use effective birth control during treatment and for 4 months after the last dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Do not breastfeed during treatment or for 4 months after your last dose.
- **Driving:** Tiredness or weakness may affect driving. Avoid driving or using machines if you feel unwell.
- **Liver:** Liver tests are done regularly. A lower dose is used in moderate liver disease. It is not advised in severe liver disease.
- **Doctor Advice:** Take it exactly as prescribed. Report bleeding, severe diarrhoea or high blood pressure to your doctor right away.
